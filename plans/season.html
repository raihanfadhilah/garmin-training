The recurring problem

You train in the grey zone by accident. On 22 August, 81.6% of moving time sat in HR 155–168, with only 14% at or below 155. That is unstructured tempo replacing easy running — the fatigue cost of hard work without the adaptation, and the reason your one quality day per week has been arriving on tired legs.

The instruction is not "start slower." Your first six kilometres are usually textbook. It is hold the opening pace for the whole run.

What the data could not tell us, and why

A week of analysis produced four different answers on whether you are improving — +13%, then −8%, then flat, then +5%. Each correction fixed a real methodological flaw, and the exercise is worth recording because it changes how the rest of this document should be read.

  • The optical heart-rate sensor under-reads for the first three to five minutes. On 28 June it recorded 120 bpm while you ran 6:47/km. Averages built on that are contaminated, and the contamination is worse on some runs than others.
  • Garmin's API returns roughly half your data by default. Streams were being downsampled from 2,607 points to 1,313, and the interpolation invented speed spikes that looked like GPS faults. Fixed — all 39 activities now stored at full 1 Hz resolution.
  • Run-to-run scatter swamps the trend. Regressed against time across eleven weeks, almost every metric returns R² between 0.01 and 0.06. Only Garmin's own Performance Condition clears significance, and it trends downward.
  • One genuine GPS fault exists — twelve seconds on 24 August, the first run after the battery died. Everything else is clean.
Consequence

You cannot read your fitness from individual easy runs, and neither can anyone else. The 5 km time trial is the only clean measurement in the plan — one controlled effort, known distance, no sensor, no windows, no inference. Everything before it is training, not data.

120 kg for 8 × 10 m at 9–11 s, against 100 kg at 9–12 s the week before. Twenty kilos heavier and the splits got faster and more consistent. At 56 kg you need 1.03× body mass to break a stationary sled versus 0.81× to keep one rolling — not stopping is worth more to you than to anyone heavier.

The honest gap

Two vertical sessions completed in seven weeks, both around 24 minutes, against a plan that prescribes 55 rising to 80. The ladder is not there, and the sessions keep dying at 20:40 on a Thursday after work.

The fix is not willpower. Either shorten them to 25–30 minutes done consistently, or move them to a weekend morning. And the StairMaster gives zero descent — it builds climbing endurance while missing the stimulus the session exists for.

Where the time is

Of an 86.5-minute median finish, 51.2 minutes is running and 32.8 is stations. Wall balls produce the highest heart rate, lactate and RPE of any station.

Doubles rules that matter

One partner works the sled at a time, the other walks behind. Stations split you-go-I-go at self-selected points. Chalk only at sled pull and farmers carry — two minutes anywhere else. Missing a sled lane is a DQ.

Post-ultra reality

Vertical jump stays depressed for 18 days after an ultra. The first three to four weeks of the Hyrox block will be compromised, so it is realistically seven or eight productive weeks, not ten. That is what validates building speed in September and October rather than waiting for November.

06 — Fuelling

Fuelling

The largest single risk in eight months is not an injury mechanism or a bad session. It is eating too little at 56 kg while volume rises.

Floor2,250

kcal before exercise is counted — ≥45 kcal per kg fat-free mass. Four days at 30 versus 45 suppressed bone formation markers in an RCT, and high protein did not rescue it.

Peak day4,800

to 5,000 kcal on a four-hour session day. Under-fuelling the peak weekend is the most likely route to a stress fracture.

The test±0 kg

Weigh weekly, same conditions. Body mass must not trend down. If it does, you are under-eating whatever any calculation says.

Session fuelling — one recipe, scaled

The base unit · 500 mL flask

25 g maltodextrin + 25 g table sugar + ⅛ tsp salt. That is 50 g of carbohydrate at 2:1 glucose:fructose, ~290 mg sodium, and it costs about a tenth of commercial mix. Race concentration is 33 g + 33 g.

Three rules that matter more than the numbers

Start at 20 minutes, not when you feel like it — you cannot catch up once behind. Set a watch alarm every 15 minutes, because everyone forgets. Every gel gets 200 mL of water — a 40 g gel taken with a few sips sits at roughly 27% concentration against 6% isotonic, and that is the stomach sting.

Porridge, properly

Everything in the bowl at the start: 80 g oats, 300 mL volle melk, a pinch of salt, half a banana mashed in. Two minutes, stir hard for twenty seconds, one to two minutes more. Peanut butter stirred all the way through, honey and the rest of the banana on top. ~700 kcal, ~95 g carbohydrate, four minutes, microwave. Use a big bowl — it climbs.

Salt at the start makes the oats taste toasted and the banana taste sweeter. Banana mashed in from the beginning dissolves and sweetens the whole bowl instead of leaving sweet pockets.

08 — Evidence

What the evidence actually says

ClaimVerdictWhat the research shows
Keto or carnivore is better for enduranceContradictedBurke's race-walker studies: high-carb +4.8%, keto −2.3%, replicated. Fat costs ~7% more oxygen per unit energy. Carbohydrate restriction impairs bone formation more than low energy availability does — directly relevant given the RED-S risk.
Zone 3 is junk milesHalf trueNo intensity distribution is definitively superior; the Norwegian model deliberately runs ~25% of volume at threshold. What is junk is accidental Zone 3 — easy runs drifting up, collecting fatigue without adaptation. That is your situation, not a training-model question.
Grip strength predicts Hyrox performanceNot supportedNo significant correlation with finish time in the peer-reviewed data. Dead hangs fix a demonstrated carry limiter for you specifically — they do not buy general race time.
Higher cadence improves running economyOverstatedStrong evidence for reduced braking and impact loading; weak-to-negative for economy in the short term. It is injury insurance and a mechanical fix, not free speed. Expect a small economy cost while it beds in.
Seed oils cause inflammationNot supported15 then 30 RCTs: no significant change across 11 inflammatory markers including CRP. Cochrane, ~59,000 participants: replacing saturated with polyunsaturated fat cut cardiovascular events 21%.

09 — Standing orders

The rules

Easy means easy

141–160 bpm, 7:15–7:35/km. If you can speak a full sentence you are in Zone 2; three or four words means you have drifted. Hold the opening pace for the whole run.

Cadence 176

Alert on, every easy run. Nine days took you from 166 to 176–181 and ground contact from 265 to 255 ms. Do not lose it.

One hard day

Monday. Everything else is easy or strength. Nothing in the middle until February, when Zone 3 becomes race-specific for the 70.3 run.

Sunday is rest

Actually rest. The 69 km ride on 23 August cost more than it built — readiness went 40 to 1, and it was the single largest training load in the file.

Weigh weekly

Same conditions. Below 54.5 kg means cut training, not increase food — especially through Ramadan, where a fasting month plus training reliably costs 1–2 kg.

Warm up before you record

Ten minutes easy before the watch starts, and wet the H10's electrodes every time. Sync the watch the night before the time trial.

Never swim alone

Buddy and tow buoy, every open-water session, no exceptions. Dry clothes and something hot ready before you get in — afterdrop peaks ten minutes after you exit.

Split the double days

Wednesday and Thursday are cardio plus upper body. Morning and evening rather than a two-hour block — each session gets a fresh you, and the evening block finishes before nine.
